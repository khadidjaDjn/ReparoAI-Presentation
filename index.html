01 · Motivation

When electronics fail,
diagnosis comes first.

Failures are ambiguous

A single symptom can correspond to several possible causes or components.

Knowledge is fragmented

Diagnostic knowledge is scattered across repair guides, videos and technical resources.

Experts spend time searching

Technicians consult multiple sources before deciding what to test or repair.

03 / 22
02 · Problem

The same symptom
can hide different faults.

Natural-language descriptions are often incomplete, informal and ambiguous. The system must connect what the technician says with the underlying diagnostic knowledge — mostly consumer electronics, since the corpus is 98% phone-related.

"The phone doesn't turn on."
"The screen stays black."
"It stopped charging."
"No response after pressing power."

20 · Conclusion

The key lesson: retrieval matters.

01 · KNOWLEDGE

A diagnostic layer can be constructed

Repair content from iFixit and YouTube was transformed into a normalised diagnostic corpus and knowledge graph.

02 · RETRIEVAL

Structured evidence improves retrieval

GraphRAG reached 90% Recall@5 compared with 74% for Hybrid RAG.

03 · DIAGNOSIS

Better retrieval improves diagnosis

GraphRAG achieved 81–84% top-1 accuracy, consistently outperforming Hybrid RAG.

Limitations

Smartphone-dominated corpus, iFixit diagnoses inferred rather than observed, modest eval set, retrieval-bounded generation, English-only.

Future work

Broader device & board-level coverage, multilingual retrieval, multimodal evidence, larger benchmark, fine-tuned generation.
