OUTLINE02 / 31
01 · INTRODUCTION  ·  CONTEXT
Fault diagnosis in electronic systems depends on expert knowledge and time-consuming consultation of technical documentation.
Faults are ambiguous and soft faults hide within normal component tolerances — raw measurements do not carry the knowledge needed.
LLMs offer a way to automate the task, but their tendency to hallucinate unsupported information limits their reliability.
GAP 1
Existing LLM-based methods target mechanical and industrial systems — bearings, motors, CNC machines, aircraft. Board-level electronic diagnosis remains largely unexplored.
GAP 2
Among hybrid RAG + KG systems, few isolate the contribution of each grounding mechanism, leaving it unclear whether gains come from retrieval, the graph, or their interaction.
PROBLÉ-
MATIQUE
How can a language model be grounded in existing repair knowledge so that it produces a reliable and traceable diagnosis for consumer electronic devices — and which grounding mechanism, document retrieval or an explicit knowledge graph, actually carries that reliability?
CONTEXT & MOTIVATION03 / 31
01 · INTRODUCTION  ·  CONTRIBUTIONS

Four contributions

01
A diagnostic dataset from public sources
Two LLM pipelines reconstruct symptom-to-cause reasoning from iFixit guides and YouTube tutorials, each validated against a manually annotated reference set — about 2,300 normalised cases.
02
A knowledge graph for electronics diagnosis
Devices, symptoms, diagnoses and the relationships between them, designed to support explicit graph traversal and to integrate with retrieval.
03
Two retrieval strategies for grounding
A hybrid RAG pipeline and a GraphRAG approach, implemented as alternative occupants of the same slot in the pipeline, so that either can be swapped in without changing anything around it.
04
A controlled comparative evaluation
Retrieval quality, diagnosis accuracy and faithfulness measured separately, across three generation backbones under identical conditions.

02 · SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE  ·  § 3.6

Grounded generation — and when to ask instead

The role is narrow by design: the model reads the assembled evidence and expresses a grounded answer. All diagnostic evidence originates in retrieval.
GRAPHRAG  ·  CLOSED-SET CONSTRAINT
The model selects from the candidate set the graph retrieved and cites the guides supporting its choice. Any diagnosis outside those candidates is by construction ungrounded.
HYBRID RAG  ·  PASSAGE-SUPPORTED
Passages are returned rather than an enumerated candidate set; the diagnosis must be supported by them and cite them. Retrieval ranking is the only backing the model has.

03 · DATA  ·  § 2.5.5 — 2.6.3

Making the corpus trustworthy

OUTPUT VALIDATION
Parsing
Code fences stripped, JSON parsed; on failure a brace-matching pass recovers the first valid object. Lists deduplicated case-insensitively.
Retries
Up to three attempts at rising temperature; guides that still fail are logged and skipped rather than halting the run.
Self-consistency
Each guide generated twice (T = 0.3 and 0.7) and the symptom sets compared by Jaccard similarity. Below 0.34 the record is flagged low-confidence.
Checkpointed and resumable, keyed by stable guide IDs and a schema version. Exclusions and failures are logged separately, so the coverage of the dataset is auditable.

Hybrid RAG

The first occupant of the retrieval slot — the corpus treated as a collection of documents, searched by meaning and by exact term at once.

04 · HYBRID RAG  ·  § 3.4.1 — 3.4.3

Two signals, one ranking

DENSE RETRIEVAL
Semantic similarity
BGE-large-en-v1.5 over faiss. Embeddings computed once at indexing time; an instruction prefix on the query only, L2-normalised so inner product is cosine.
Blind spot: blurs error codes and model numbers.
SPARSE RETRIEVAL
Exact technical terms
BM25 over the same chunks. Matches identifiers, error codes and part names literally, by construction rather than by approximation.
Blind spot: misses paraphrase entirely.
RRF(d) =
∑
1
k + rankr(d)
(3.1)
Fusion needs no comparable raw scores — necessary, since cosine similarity and BM25 are measured on different scales. k flattens the curve so small rank differences near the top of one list do not dominate the fused ranking. Each retriever is queried for a pool larger than the final result count, so a chunk ranked highly by one but just outside the other’s top results can still surface.
Corpus preparation (§ 3.4.1)  — each case is split into a diagnosis chunk and a repair chunk, embedded and indexed independently, so “what could be wrong?” and “how do I fix it?” retrieve different things while sharing a case identifier.

WHY SCHEMA-FIRST
The diagnostic domain has recurring concepts, so fixed Symptom and Diagnosis types limit the LLM to a defined vocabulary instead of open extraction — reducing the surface-form variability that would fragment shared nodes.
PHASE 1 · INGESTION
One MERGE-based transaction per record, each node keyed on a unique property — idempotent by construction, and shared symptoms converge onto single nodes.
PHASE 2 · AGGREGATION
Co-occurrence edges deleted and rebuilt in full, so re-tagged or removed guides leave no stale weights behind.

3.5.4 TRAVERSAL19 / 31
05 · GRAPHRAG  ·  § 3.5.5

Guide Scoring and Diagnosis Aggregation

GUIDE SCORE
score(g)= ∑s ∈ Sgsim(q, s) ∑s ∈ Lsim(q, s) (3.5)
The similarity mass of the symptoms guide g matches, normalised by the total linking mass of the query. Matching all reported symptoms scores one; matching part scores proportionally lower — rewarding guides that account for the full combination rather than a symptom in isolation.
DIAGNOSIS EVIDENCE
ev(d)= ∑g : d ∈ Dg score(g)|Dg| (3.6)
Each guide contributes its score as evidence for the diagnosis it addresses, and guides addressing the same diagnosis accumulate. A guide listing several diagnoses expresses uncertainty among them, so its evidence is divided equally rather than counted in full for each. Aggregated scores are normalised to sum to one.

Conclusion

Findings, limitations, and the directions they point to.

07 · CONCLUSION  ·  FINDINGS

Three conclusions

01
Hybrid RAG retrieves reliably
Assessed in isolation, it performs on realistically worded queries and covers the full fault taxonomy.
02
GraphRAG is consistently ahead
Across all three generation backbones, with full faithfulness to the retrieved candidate set and near-perfect citation validity.
03
Retrieval coverage decides
Not the language model, nor the encoder: within a fixed retrieval stage, a larger backbone yields only marginal gains.
The lever for further improvement is the structure and coverage of the evidence surfaced to the model.
FINDINGS28 / 31
07 · CONCLUSION  ·  LIMITATIONS

What the work does not establish

01
The corpus is dominated by smartphone cases, so coverage of other categories — and of true board-level circuits — is thin.
02
The iFixit diagnostic layer is inferred from repair procedures rather than observed directly — a gap the confidence flag marks but does not remove.
03
The held-out evaluation set is manually verified but modest in size.
04
The output is retrieval-limited: the assistant returns the closest matching repair guide rather than generating a procedure of its own.
05
The pipeline is English-only, so a query phrased in another language falls outside what either strategy can match.
AND ONE CAVEAT ON THE SCORE
The normalised graph score is a heuristic, not a calibrated probability. Scores are non-negative and sum to one, but they are not the output of a probabilistic model and have not been calibrated against observed diagnostic frequencies.

Confidence is read from the leader’s separation from the runner-up, not from its absolute value.

Raising the retrieval ceiling

EVIDENCE BASE
Broaden the corpus and graph to more device categories and to genuine circuit-level data.
RETRIEVAL

BACKUP  ·  EVALUATION VALIDITY

Is the evaluation circular?

HELD OUT OF THE GRAPH
The 50 GraphRAG evaluation cases are excluded from the knowledge graph. Their diagnoses must be reached from other cases, so the measurement is generalisation — not retrieval of a case’s own answer.
HAND-LABELLED QUERIES
The retrieval evaluation uses 50 user-phrased queries, written the way a person describes a fault rather than copied from corpus text, each paired with a manually verified set of acceptable chunks.
VALIDATED BEFORE TRUSTED
Each extraction stage was checked against a manual reference before use: 30 manually extracted videos for the transcript model, 50 manually tagged guides for the tagger (92%).
REPRODUCIBILITY
Decoding is deterministic, so the reported numbers are reproducible. The corpus run is checkpointed and keyed by stable guide IDs plus a schema version.
UNCERTAINTY IS MARKED, NOT HIDDEN
13.4% of records (270) carry a low-confidence flag from the self-consistency check; exclusions and failures are logged separately.
What remains true: the gold diagnosis tags originate in the same inferred layer, so the evaluation measures whether the system recovers the knowledge the corpus encodes — not whether that knowledge matches bench measurements on real boards. That is limitation 02, and closing it needs observed diagnostic data rather than a different metric.

Dense and Sparse Retrieval

Dense

BGE-large-en-v1.5 (Xiao et al., 2023)
Embeddings computed once at indexing time
Asymmetric convention: instruction prefix on the query, not on indexed passages
L2-normalised, so inner product ≡ cosine similarity
Exact inner-product faiss index — at ~10³ chunks, ANN would trade accuracy for an unneeded speed-up

Sparse

Okapi BM25 (Robertson & Zaragoza, 2009)
Indexed over a targeted field subset: symptom tags, recorded user phrasings, problem description, device identifiers
Full chunk text dilutes that vocabulary with root causes and diagnostic questions
Device tokens repeated so rare, discriminating terms compete with shared symptom vocabulary
Deliberately simple tokenisation — exact-token matching, not linguistic analysis

03 · METHODOLOGY  ·  § 3.4.3

Reciprocal Rank Fusion

Merges two rankings without requiring their raw scores to be comparable — necessary, since cosine similarity and BM25 are measured on different scales.

RRF(d)=  ∑r ∈ {dense, sparse} 1k + rankr(d)
(3.1)
k flattens the score curve, so small rank differences near the top of a single list do not dominate the fused ranking.
Each retriever is queried for a pool larger than the final result count, so a chunk ranked highly by one retriever but just outside the other's top results can still surface.
