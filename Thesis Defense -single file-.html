Introduction

01 · INTRODUCTION  ·  CONTEXT

Context

Fault diagnosis in electronic boards and modules depends on expert knowledge and time-consuming consultation of technical documentation.
Large language models could automate the task, but they state unsupported causes with full confidence — and a wrong diagnosis means a wrong repair.
THE QUESTION
How can a language model be grounded in real repair knowledge, so that its diagnoses are supported by evidence?

02 · DATA & KG  ·  § 2.5.5 — 2.5.6

Validation, reliability, reproducibility

PARSING
Code fences stripped, JSON parsed; on failure a brace-matching pass finds the first valid object. Lists normalised and deduplicated case-insensitively.
RETRIES
Up to three attempts with rising temperature; guides that still fail are logged and skipped rather than halting the corpus run.
SELF-CONSISTENCY
Each guide generated twice (T = 0.3 and 0.7); symptom sets compared by Jaccard similarity. Below 0.34 the record is marked low-confidence and the union kept as candidates.
Checkpointed and resumable: stable guide identifiers plus a schema version, so records under an old schema are regenerated rather than silently mixed.
Exclusions and failures logged separately, making the coverage and limitations of the dataset auditable.

WHY SCHEMA-FIRST

Knowledge Graph Enrichment

Every symptom and diagnosis node carries a canonical tag, e.g. not_charging.
A short textual representation combines the readable tag with its curated synonyms and alternative user phrasings.
That text is encoded into a dense vector and stored on the node, alongside the text it came from.
Free-text queries then map to canonical tags without exact keyword overlap. Performed once at build time.

03 · METHODOLOGY  ·  § 3.5.2

Entity Linking

sim(q, n)=cos(vq, vn)= vq · vn‖vq‖ ‖vn‖ (3.2)
sim(q, n)=v̂q · v̂n (3.3)
The query is encoded with the same model used to enrich the nodes, so both lie in a common representation space.
Compared against every symptom node by cosine similarity; the most similar are linked. Unit-length embeddings reduce this to a dot product.
A threshold discards weak matches; a cap limits how many symptoms one query may link.
Links keep their similarity scores rather than being binary — a query commonly reports several symptoms, and the discriminating evidence lies in their combination.

3.5.4 TRAVERSAL30 / 48
03 · METHODOLOGY  ·  § 3.5.5

Guide Scoring and Diagnosis Aggregation

GUIDE SCORE
score(g)= ∑s ∈ Sgsim(q, s) ∑s ∈ Lsim(q, s) (3.5)
The similarity mass of the symptoms guide g matches, normalised by the total linking mass of the query. Matching all reported symptoms scores one; matching part scores proportionally lower — rewarding guides that account for the full combination rather than a symptom in isolation.
DIAGNOSIS EVIDENCE
ev(d)= ∑g : d ∈ Dg score(g)|Dg| (3.6)
Each guide contributes its score as evidence for the diagnosis it addresses, and guides addressing the same diagnosis accumulate. A guide listing several diagnoses expresses uncertainty among them, so its evidence is divided equally rather than counted in full for each. Aggregated scores are normalised to sum to one.

03 · METHODOLOGY  ·  § 3.5.5 — NOTE

Confidence Signal

The normalised score is a graph-derived heuristic, not a calibrated probability.

Scores are non-negative and sum to one, but are not the output of a probabilistic model and have not been calibrated against observed diagnostic frequencies.
Confidence is read from the leader's separation from the runner-up, not from its absolute score.
Closely-ranked candidates are the signal on which the clarification path acts.

03 · METHODOLOGY  ·  § 3.6

Evidence-Grounded Generation

The role is deliberately narrow: the model reads the assembled evidence and expresses a grounded answer. All diagnostic evidence originates in retrieval.

GRAPHRAG
Closed-set constraint
The model selects from the candidate set retrieved by the graph and cites the guides supporting its choice. Any diagnosis outside those candidates is by construction ungrounded.
HYBRID RAG
Passage-supported
Passages are returned rather than an enumerated candidate set; the diagnosis must be supported by the retrieved passages and cite them. Retrieval ranking is the only backing the model has.

Conclusion

Findings, limitations, and the directions they point to.

05 · CONCLUSION  ·  LIMITATIONS

What the work does not establish

01
The corpus is dominated by smartphone cases, so coverage of other categories — and of true board-level circuits — is thin.
02
The iFixit diagnostic layer is inferred from repair procedures rather than observed directly — a gap the confidence flag marks but does not remove.
03
The held-out evaluation set is manually verified but modest in size.
04
The output is retrieval-limited: the assistant returns the closest matching repair guide rather than generating a procedure of its own.
05
The pipeline is English-only, so a query phrased in another language falls outside what either strategy can match.
LIMITATIONS46 / 48
05 · CONCLUSION  ·  FUTURE WORK

Raising the retrieval ceiling

EVIDENCE BASE
Broaden the corpus and graph to more device categories and to genuine circuit-level data.
RETRIEVAL
Multilingual queries, multimodal evidence such as schematics and board images, and graph reasoning beyond symptom-to-diagnosis traversal.
GENERATION
Fine-tune on the repair corpus so the assistant can generate repair guidance directly, not only retrieve the nearest existing guide.
On the evaluation side, scaling to a larger benchmark would strengthen confidence in the reported results.
